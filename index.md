## Biography
I joined the Department of Statistics at the University of Manitoba as an Assistant Professor in September 2025. Prior to that, I completed my Ph.D. in Statistics at the University of British Columbia, where I was supervised by [Dr. Harry Joe](https://www.stat.ubc.ca/~harry/){:target="_blank"}. My doctoral research, funded by the NSERC Doctoral Scholarship and the Scotiabank Risk Analytics Initiative, focused on statistical inference for multivariate extremes with applications to systemic risk in financial systems.


I graduated in June 2025 with the Marshall Prize for Excellence in Statistics awarded by the department of Statistics [\[Award announcement\]](https://www.stat.ubc.ca/news/xiaoting-li-wins-2024-25-marshall-prize-excellence-statistics){:target="_blank"} and the Lorraine Schwartz Prize in Probability awarded by the department of Mathematics [\[Award announcement\]](https://www.stat.ubc.ca/news/xiaoting-li-awarded-2025-lorraine-schwartz-prize){:target="_blank"}. In 2026, I received the Pierre Robillard Award from the Statistical Society of Canada, which recognizes the best PhD thesis in statistics or probability defended at a Canadian university. [\[Award News\]](https://ssc.ca/en/awards/2026/xiaoting-li-2026-pierre-robillard-award-winner){:target="_blank"}


Prior to my doctoral studies, I obtained a Bachelor’s degree in Joint Honours Economics and Finance from McGill University,
graduating with the Allen Oliver Gold Medal. Motivated by a strong interest in quantitative research, I continued my graduate training in Statistics under the supervison of [Dr. Christian Genest](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/cgenest/){:target="_blank"}, who introduced me to extreme value theory and guided my early research on the modeling of clustered extremes for hydrological applications.
